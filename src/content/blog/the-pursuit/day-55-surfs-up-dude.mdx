This last week I’ve been battling a cold, strangely enough it seems worse in the mornings and evenings. I tend to forget about it during the day. It’s definitely a motivation killer when your looking for camping spots which means the warmth of a bed seems even more appealing.

## Day 52
I felt like death warmed up when my alarmed buzzed early in the morning. Despite having spent the night in a really decent hostel I still managed to get a cold. It might have had something to do with the fact that the heating was on full blast while I tried to dry my clothes, which I washed in the shower to save the 6 euro fee.

Being sick it seemed like a good idea to have soup for breakfast, so I heated up some left overs and toasted some bread. Rules are made to be broken, right?!

Marks from an earlier safa found in the hostel rafters
By the time I had packed up my gear and got ready to set off it was 11:30 and another late start for me. These were becoming a habit, although without any real to need hurry I figured ‘late is better than never‘. The weather was a little strange being dry one minute only for there to be a heavy downpour the next. I suited up with my rain gear and after a quick stop at the ‘playa POO‘ I recommenced my pilgrimage.

There was a noticeable wobble in Zen’s bottom bracket which was cause for concern. There had been a few cringe worthy knocks and bumps while I climbed a fence getting out of a nature reserve a few days early and I thought I might have caused some damage. Passing by a local mechanic I decided to stop and get it checked out. Not so much for the expertise but more because I didn’t have a large enough spanner to remove the crank side bearing cup.

Low and behold they spoke no English at all which gave me a fantastic opportunity to practice my Spanish. Despite having learnt some Spanish a few years earlier while spending time volunteering around the country I never really learnt any words relating to bicycles. It was an interesting attempt at conversation but we got their in the end. Embarrassingly he also noticed that I had put my front tire on the wrong way round and the tread was causing some drag. No doubt this was the last time I changed the inner tube after 4 hours of sleep and a cold night.

Being sick I wasn’t particularly motivated and this meant a slow cycle. After walking a really steep hill I stopped near the top for some peanut butter banana sandwiches and a fantastic view. To my detriment the route was full of uphills which on the day meant lots of walking and less cycling.

Peanut butter, banana and avocado sandwich. Yum!
A really heavy downpour had me seek shelter and a ‘cafe solo’ were I planned my sleeping arrangements for the night. I chose another hostel hoping to fend off the cold that had decided to join me for the ride. It was a short distance away and despite the sun setting I thought I could make it before it got dark. Alas the route was mostly off road, all muddy and extremely slippery.

When I eventually made it to the hostel I had to wake the owner who let me inside for 4 euro. It was a peaceful albergue with no internet and I had time to reflect. I was alone, inline with the theme of this trip (sense the frustration).

## Day 53
Being a true albergue I had to be up and out early in the morning which meant I was up at 7 am on the dot. I cooked up my regular breakfast of oats and coffee and packed up most of my gear leaving out some tools and parts for Zen.

I had been treating a minor squeek/crack in the front hub with some lube and decided it was a good time to strip it and grease it properly. Upon further investigation I had left it too long and the bearings were already damaged. My spares were not the right size which meant I had no choice but to grease everything up as best I could and carry on. No harm done as I figure I am going to need a new wheel soon anyhow.

This was a special day for me as it was my one year anniversary since quitting alcohol. A toxic relationship that I never truly understood until after I had ended it. This meant I had to spoil myself. Even though I had already had my sandwiches for lunch I stopped in a restaurant for a second lunch of ‘patatas bravas’ before heading into the next major city on the route, Gijon.

Hill top view
After hanging around the beach for the sunset and still feeling slightly sick (excuses) I decided to book into a hotel. Why not really treat myself after all?! The room wasn’t fancy but it had a bath and after adding in the complimentary liquid soap and shampoo I had a bubble bath, I was grinning from ear to ear.

I gave myself a haircut with my beard trimmer and set off for dinner in a nearby vegan spot. I really love it when I am able to find vegan junk food! Check out the Puzzle Bar if you are ever in the area.

It seemed quiet out and so I headed back to the hotel. I managed to figure out how to change the TV’s sound to English and got to enjoy a movie in my home language before dozing off. It’s funny because something like this two months ago would have been a less than ideal situation that I had to endure. Whereas now this was pure heaven, I loved every second of this newfound luxury.

## Day 54
Taking advantage of the 12 pm checkout I laid in bed messing around on the internet until 11:30 before packing up and getting back on the road. This was the only place for another 70 km that I could buy camping gas so I took a 30 minute detour to do just that. On arriving I found the place closed, with a sign on the door saying they should be open. Just another one of those things I guess, plus I had been thinking of trying out these DIY alcohol stoves.

A little demotivated by having to come back the same way empty handed I stopped for a coffee when I got back onto my route. The ride was fairly easy compared to earlier days, although I’m not sure if this was just because I was recovering and better able to climb the constant hills.

I bumped into a familiar peregrino while resting in one of the towns and we chatted for a while before he went in search of Mary Jane. This was now the third time we bumped into each other and he was walking which is testament to my slow cycling, or maybe his fast walking?!

A town named after a close friend (after some modifications)
While using google maps to search for a suitable camping spot near the beach I stumbled upon an albergue with really, really good reviews (‘Unmissable’, ‘Best albergue on the path’) and decided it was worth a night there. Perhaps I was becoming precious with all these nights indoors. On the way I crossed paths with another peregrino, Kang, who I had not met and he told me he was staying in a surf hostel near the beach. This seemed exciting so I said I would join him if the albergue I was intending on visiting was closed.

After 4 phone calls with no answer and waiting 30 minutes I headed to the surf hostel. The weather was going to be really good the next day so I paid for two nights. Words cannot express how excited I was to spend the next day on the beach in the sun.

The local bar ‘La Luna‘ served some vegan options which was a treat and Kang and myself enjoyed a a fun night out with good music and good food.

## Day 55
It felt good waking up knowing that a day of doing nothing lay ahead. We ate some breakfast and had some coffee while watching some surf films. The plan was to hit the waves and we wanted to get some inspiration. The earliest we would get access to our rented boards and wet suits was 12 pm so we had some fun on the slack-line while enjoying the sunshine.

It was smiles all round as we hit the beach before grabbing some lunch. The day was just too perfect to describe. This was the second time so far that I have felt truly at home on this trip, the first being while crossing over to Santander on the ferry. I’m not sure what this means but I’m not oblivious to the fact that both of these days were spent on the water.

Tomorrow its back on the Camino, as Kang and I part ways. My spirits are high and I feel as though I am overflowing with joy. I wish you all the same feelings.

Until next time, love and light!
