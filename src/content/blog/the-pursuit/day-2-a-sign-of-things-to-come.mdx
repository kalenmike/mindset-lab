## A Stormy Awakening

Sleeping was near impossible! The wind howled like a hungry wolf while the rain punished the polyester cover we called home. Outside I could hear the flapping of a loose tarp; which kept me from getting any deep rest. There was no chance I was going outside in that weather! I’m sure you can relate. Mostimes Sometimes I’d rather just put up with annoyance rather than leave my bed. Patience or laziness? You tell me.

This being the first night I had some trouble trusting things would be ok. The loudest thought in my mind was that our gear and bicycles would be snatched away by a silent thief. And to be honest with the noise going on outside, he wouldn’t even need to be that silent. Maybe trust is something I will learn from this passage.

## The Morning Rush

The alarm rang at 6 am. Despite being tired I welcomed the noise. No more pretending trying to sleep! We had one hour to pack up our gear. Another two hours were planned in for our cycle to the port. With our ferry leaving at 10 am that should have been sufficient.

It was warm enough in the tent which made getting dressed easier. Outside was another story. The cold was in my bones and the rain caressed my now wet clothes (waterproofs for the win!). I could sense the annoyance in my companions aura body language.

Aha! You know that tail wagging I told you about yesterday? It was caused by a missing bolt on Zen’s (my mechanical steed) rear rack. And the mystery is solved. I love it when a troubling problem ends with a simple solution. I dug out a replacement and just like that sorted (I’m handy like that).

With the bikes loaded, we set off. I was optimistic, after all, it was the first day. This view was not shared and I did my best to limit our conversation. Only one day in from a comfortable London flat and waking up indoors is replaced by the cold, rain and wind. Who can blame him!

We’re late, late for an important date! The cycle was a hurried mess. The strong wind threw us around the road and google maps got us lost. We just didn’t want to be outside any longer. Our tickets were only £25 but as they were non-refundable we didn’t want to miss boarding. You could call it money practice for the road. #thrifty

Getting thrown in the deep end like this meant we faced some apprehension about continuing onward. Rather than stop, we decided to talk about it at breakfast. On the ferry, in the warmth, over a coffee (cannot wait).

---

## Crossing the Channel

Yeah ok, so we were a little late. But crucially late for boarding and not for departure. After checking with her boss, the women at the desk let us board. I heard him say “they were exposed to the elements” (love you, bro). We followed a painted line which led us where we needed to be.

Bikes on board, balancing carefully in the hold we head upstairs for breakfast. Ha-lle-lu-jah! The vegetarian breakfast became vegan by dropping the eggs and we sat down to eat. I shouldn’t leave out the coffee, it wasn’t great but the warmth was well needed. It soothed our aching bones.

Once satisfied and warm it was time to discuss our future. Personally, I was happy to continue on. After sharing how hard the morning had been, his doubts and fears we both agreed on the same. We did, however, make one alteration, our route. Instead of going coastal we would go inland. We thought the weather might be better.

I had only ever been seasick once in my life, this made it twice. I say seasick but really I mean little queasy. Sleeping sorted me out (like it does for most things). The waters were really rough to be fair. Our neighbours, a school group, weren’t so lucky and the floor quickly changed colour.

## Becoming Tourists

We carefully returned to our bikes after docking. Taking great care to avoid any area of the floor that was not the same colour it had been when we boarded.

Slight confusion ensued while we were reminded that the French drive on the other side of the road (whoops!). It was still raining and we didn’t really know what to do next. After visiting the tourist information centre we checked into the cheapest hotel in town, with available beds. The room was plain and simple, and just what we needed. Make it to Calais, check!

Wow, I’m not entirely sure I expected our journey to pan out this way. Perhaps it was not well thought out. Or maybe we are not well enough equipped to handle such a grand undertaking. I can only hope that each passing day gets a little easier. Plus if you face the most difficult situations to start everything after it is a picnic, right?! #WhenInDoubtOptimismsTheShout
