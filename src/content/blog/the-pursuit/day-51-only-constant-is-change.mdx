I can sense changes in myself, all this time on the road is altering me at my very core. I had imagined before leaving on this journey that it would be a series of unfolding answers as I slowly but surely uncovered the secrets I was searching for. Every kilometre closer I got to Lisbon would reveal another secret guiding me in the direction I should live out the rest of my life. I wouldn’t have to do anything but commit to the trip and the rest would just show up when needed. If only life were so easy.

I often wonder what I spent my days thinking about, my mind is still scattered and I don’t feel any closer to the answers I was hoping to find. The difference is that what I worry about has changed. I find myself anxious about where I will be sleeping, where my water refill will be coming from or if I’m getting enough calories so as not to lose weight. Lately since joining the Camino de Santiago acquiring my daily stamps has been added to this list. The many years I spent living in the city has made a habit of anxiety. I feel it is time to take a step back, the answers I seek don’t lie in this journey itself, rather the journey is only a means to help concentrate on finding them.

When I last checked in I was on my way out of a youth hostel and expecting to spend the night wild camping, instead I ended up spending so much time working on my blog that it got late and I opted to try my first Albergue. I imagined it would be filled with many other pilgrims who would share their knowledge of the camino and guide me further along my path. Instead I found hip hop music and the now typical western culture wannabe gangsta’s. It was a draining experience and I was eager to leave and get back to being alone or at least away from this perfect example of consumerism and modern culture. I wanted something spiritual, something wholesome, with meaning or purpose not another MTV episode.

The next day despite the rain I was happy to be on the road. It was a tough cycle with some hills that I just couldn’t manage and I had to walk some. It takes so much more energy pushing the bike up a hill, the handle bars are low which forces you to bend over slightly and the weight pulls you back down the hill at even the slightest pause. The weather is getting warmer and my winter clothes are now too bulky and hot to wear most of the time which means Zen is looking more loaded than normal. It’s that awkward in between stage and I’m not quite sure if I need to dress for cold or warm weather, instead I focus on staying dry.

I found my way to a beautifully secluded spot over looking the ocean and decided it wasn’t worth passing up on the opportunity to stay there. Excited about the prospect of trying my new hammock I found some trees and unpacked. I slept like a baby that night, it was truly the most comfortable I have been since leaving my Egyptian cotton sheets behind in London. The next morning I was joined by many day hikers and I realised the spot was not so secluded after all, but no one seemed to mind my presence as I ate breakfast and got ready to leave.

The path forward was off road, muddy, rocky and slippery. I pushed Zen until I hit tarmac. I managed to get a few kilometres further before I was abruptly stopped on an uphill and I looked back to discover that my rear derailleur had snapped! I can’t say I was surprised or even upset, I am getting used to things going wrong. They go wrong just as much as they go right and that’s becoming ok with me. I converted Zen into a fixie by removing what was left of the derailleur and shortening the chain, opting for a lower gear ratio to save me from the hills. It was a hard ride, much harder than I imagined but I eventually made it to a local bike mechanic who had the parts I needed to get Zen back up to full health.

I found somewhere for lunch and took my time eating before setting off to find a camp spot. Just as I passed a sign to another Albergue my rear wheel seized and it became apparent that a bolt had snapped on my pannier rack. I took it as a sign to stay in the Albergue, it wasn’t a sign. The hostel was empty and while the town was beautifully quaint, camping would have offered me more. Being a weekend all the shops were closed and I was lucky enough to have the right replacement bolts. I fixed up the bike once again before setting off the next morning.

Now this was more like it. It was pretty easy riding. The new parts on Zen made her ride with little resistance and the sun was out. I saw the most amazing coastal views and got to take a small ferry across the sea. It felt like I had been transported somewhere new, like a whole new journey. Santander was a fun city with a great vegan restaurant, something which I’m finding extremely rare in Spain.

The views on this leg of the journey have been something out of this world and its almost impossible not to stay in the moment and take it all in. I have had some of my best camp spots and finally met some fellow peregrino’s.

    

Something is changing, I’m not entirely sure what it is yet but this trip has started to evolve…
