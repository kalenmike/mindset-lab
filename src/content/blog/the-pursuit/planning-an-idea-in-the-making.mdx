Twenty-seventeen has been one heck of a year. It blows my mind the number of changes that have come my way. Smoking went out the window in January. February saw me alcohol-free. I finally began my vegan transformation in April. Trying to express all the lessons I have learnt is not even conceivable.

## Hitting Rock Bottom at Twenty-Nine

With the big ‘three O’ birthday coming in twenty-eighteen I’ve hit a quarter-life crisis. One day I just woke up and realised that I wasn’t happy. Despite all the hard work I had put in to get where I was. But change is scary so I did what every twenty-something millennial does when facing difficulties. I called dad.

<Polaroid
    image={d0Dad}
    caption="Dad, a.k.a 'Captain Tim'"
/>

## The Decision

Dad has always been very supportive of my creative whims. I think in some ways he tries to live through me. If ever I found myself unsure about something I could count on dad to motivate me into action.

Needless to say, his advice was to scrap twenty eighteen. Live the year on a whim and see where it took me. It’s hard to argue with your parents so I got started doing just that.

The first step proved most difficult. I battled opposing thoughts for days as I tried to decide what I would do. This proved futile and I eventually realised I had no idea what I wanted.

Take two meant I would list all the things I did not want. I would then make plans that did not include anything on that list. This was surprisingly easy. Who would have thought it would be easier to list what you don’t want than what you do.

It came down to two simple actions. I needed to leave London (for good) and I needed to change my career (completely). Everything else would work out if I just made these two small changes. Granted there was a whole lot of faith in that theory.



## As Luck Would Have It

It just so happened that a friend of mine was going through a similar crisis. He had just quit his job and was about to commence on a bicycle tour to Lisbon. We met for coffee and by the time I left we had made arrangements for me to drive us to Lisbon.

For a long time, I had romantic imaginings of a European road trip and this excited me. The steps that followed moved surprisingly quick. Quit job, check. Serve notice on flat, check. Notify those who need to know, check. Sell almost everything I own, check.

Impressively within one month, the remains of my possessions could fit into my 2006 Peugeot 206 and my bank balance was much heavier. I started to mentally prepare myself for life on the road. I slept on the floor. The heating stayed off and the window stayed open. My cooking was done out of one pot and I drank from the same cup.

<Polaroid
    image={d0Car}
    caption="The old trusty steed, Peugeot 206"

My notice finished before my friends. This gave me time to prepare, plan, and imagine. I took regular walks in the local forest and started practising my knots. Rubbing sticks to make fire was a challenge I have still to master. Thankfully I carry a jet flame!

In hindsight, the biggest difficulty I faced was letting go of the things I had come to hold dear. I wanted to keep some things that just wouldn’t serve me on the road. My Egyptian cotton sheets almost brought tears to my eyes when they left. While parting with my guitar was a bittersweet experience.



## Trading Luxury for Freedom

The last big item I owned was the car. All of a sudden it seemed like dead weight and I wanted it gone. My recent analysis of the fuel costs might have played a part in that decision.

If I was going to do this trip, I was going to do it right! I sold the car and bought a second-hand bicycle in its place. Second-hand felt more legitimate. I figured parts would be more widely available. And let’s face it we just used to make things better and more reliable in the past.

<Polaroid
    image={d0Possesions}
    caption="The only possessions I decided to keep."

## Embracing Minimalism

With most of my possessions gone, I feel great. Literally like I have less baggage. It is the little step before, the letting go. That really challenges you! I am now a minimalist and it feels incredible.

This is it as if I was never in London I will vanish without a trace. I am happy to have company on this trip. This adventure will bring me closer to my purpose in life, it has to.

## The Road to Lisbon

And so begins my pursuit. The current destination is Lisbon and the route is unclear. It is not where I am heading but the road to get there. That is where I expect to find the answers I am looking for.
