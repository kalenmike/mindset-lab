The road seems quiet, only the birds chirp in the distance. For a moment the stillness brings a sense of peace and calmness, then the realisation hits that Christmas is approaching. What happened to the days of December?! Pictures of the typical hallmark family, next a fireplace, opening presents flood my mind. I am alone.


Its not that I haven’t spent many Christmas alone before or at least ‘sans famille’. There is something about this whole experience that opens you up to all the questions you’ve been hiding away from for so long. I begin to wonder whether life has any meaning if it is not shared with people you love.

Leaving my motel on day 14 the sky was grey but it was dry and that’s all that mattered. My gear freshly packed and waterproofed I felt I could conquer the world. Before setting off I decided I would buy Zen that tent I had been pondering over and cycled down to purchase a full bike cover. I also picked up a ground tarp. It took a little bit of effort and organisation to repack these rather large items into my already cumbersome load but I got it done.

Setting off I ran into some minor but annoying mechanical problems which delayed me as I did my best to resolve them before moving on. My poor Zenny was taking a beating and it was only going to get worse. It was at this point that I noticed my rear rack had come apart at the weld in two locations and it is now being held together in three different places by duct tape. Remember what I said about the only two tools you ever need on Day One?

> “There are only two tools you ever need to fix any problem, WD40 (Q20) and Duct tape.” - Dad

The little delays, setbacks and late start meant I covered very little distance before seeking shelter from the fog. I found myself in a beautiful forest just as the sun was setting. Words just cannot express the way true beauty melts away your worries and fears. This was the perfect time for a little yoga before snacking on some almond paste and settling in for the night.

I honestly think I may be sleeping too much. I’m trying to be kind to my body after the punishment I’m putting it through but with the early sunsets its very easy to curl up and wake up in the middle of the night instead. I then don’t want to face the cold at 3/4am so I force myself to stay in bed till the alarm goes off. What do you think, how many hours sleep is too much?

The night was quiet apart from the hooting of owls as they surveyed their hunting grounds for breakfast. I got up on the morning of day 15 fresh and ready to go. I knew the challenge it would be to get back to the main road as I would need to backtrack through the brambles, over the river and between the dry grass I had conquered the night before. The weeds lashed onto Zen as I pushed with all my might almost as if they didn’t want us to leave. Hooking around the cogs and grabbing onto my gear it seemed like the scary forests of the old Disney moves.

When I made it to the road I found that my rear mud guard had been badly damaged and it was obstructing the spokes. A minor adjustment sorted this, hey, its only a mud guard right? Nothing mission critical and besides I was already covered in mud anyway.

I started the ride with the bold intentions of making it to Tours the same day, about 120 km away. That plan quickly changed the moment the hunger set it. I had been snacking on some cherry tomatoes and rocket, with the aim of getting some vitamins in my system but they didn’t do much for my calorie intake so I stopped at a nearby cafe for some lunch and purchased a few supplies from a local store.

I’m not sure exactly what happened after that but I found myself on a long and steep hill and somehow managed to injure my left knee. Fearing further damage and a long delay to my continued travels I kept heading South at a slow pace searching for a camp spot. I found a nice article which I hope will stop this pain in its tracks. Dinner was followed by some light stretching and then reading before bed.

Early day 16 I was up and at it, managing to pack up all my gear earlier than planned. The weather had been dry the past few days which made this task much simpler. A quick look at the forecast showed that this would change unless I could make it to Tours. Still I had braved the rains before and this forest was swarming with mushrooms so I thought I’d try my hand at mycology, the study of mushrooms.

Mushrooms are the one area where the old saying “what doesn’t kill you, makes you stronger” doesn’t really apply, but alas we have to start somewhere. I opted not to eat anything but rather to sketch out some specimens and later in the day try and identify them. Doing this I hoped to learn what characteristics are important to take note of in the future. It turns out that this is a really serious hobby and unfortunately one that can kill if you make a mistake. Challenge accepted!

The day was a hard push combined with some amazing foods. My limited choice is starting to bring out flavours in foods I never noticed before. Even something as simple as avocado on bread leaves my mouth watering. I found a public toilet on the way and had a ‘hand shower’, washed down Zen and refilled both my water bottles which aren’t seeming to last too long these days.

Camping that night was a sort of change in thinking. The forest must have been seeming with deer as I heard constant calls and barking from all directions. Even though they were distant the stomping sounded powerful and I imagined the stags fighting over dominance while onlookers cheered them on. This brought me back to a book I read when I was a child, Fire Bringer by David Clement-Davies, and I found it comforting. If you haven’t heard the different sounds that deer make before, wild camping is definitely a great yet frighting first way to do so, which I highly recommend.

With it being so obvious that these marvellous creatures where abundant in the area I began to wonder what predators may be lurking around the corner and all of sudden the thin polyester walls of my tent didn’t seem to offer any comfort. While I’m sure it wouldn’t do much against an attacking animal, nevertheless,  my trusty Leatherman stayed by my side. Appropriately engraved with the words “Never Give Up, Love Dad”

The sounds continued until the morning, mixed in with various other calls and more owls hooting. For someone like me it was heaven. For that brief moment in time, I was alive in a world that had and needed nothing else. It was genuine and wholesome. Some brief mushroom sketching and I was on my way out. Or so I thought.

Somewhere along the way I got myself all turned around and I had been pushing Zen for ages through the thick mud and leaves before realising I was lost. Of all places I have stayed this seemed close to the most wild and I was lost! It wasn’t possible to just follow my compass because of the thick forage that came up on occasion and pushing/lifting a loaded Zen over obstacles was no easy task.

I did eventually make my way out, albeit, with some damage to Zen’s rear derailer. I fixed her up enough to ride and headed to Tours, opting for main roads and the quicker routes over the quieter ones. After failing to find appropriate camp grounds and needing further repairs and parts I booked into a local motel for yet another pit stop and shelter from the violent dark. I had already witnessed two fights within hours of arriving.

It’s a strange thing looking back on the ride so far. It once seemed so mysterious and frightening but now the road is becoming my new normal. I have become used to my limited conversations and hand signals, although my French is improving with the addition of a French-English phrase book. The simple meals and lack of choice bring with it a sense of comfort. Allowing the mind to ponder over more important things. And strangely enough, aside from the minor hiccup with my knee the cycling is not that hard at all.

My greatest fear now remains that which has plagued humanity since the dawn of time, the fear of the unknown and what is coming around the corner.

I hope you are enjoying following along on my pursuit but if there is anything you would like to read more about or indeed, less about, I’d love it if you left me a comment letting me know. Until the next, Merci. Au Revoir. Prends soin de toi.
