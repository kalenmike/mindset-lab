## Day 65 – 68
This was the final push to complete my Camino de Santiago. My time on the way was spiritual and I enjoyed most of the cycle. In my early days, I had decided to treat this as a sort of rebirth. I would lay everything on the line as I headed towards the Compestella. This would be my final trek as the person I was. On finishing my journey I would be fresh and new. All my past mistakes would be forgiven. I reminded myself of this each time I faced a steep uphill when the rain soaked my socks or continuing on just seemed impossible.

I rode the 100 km into Santiago in one day. Despite this trip being spiritual to me, I was also happy for it to end so that a new chapter could begin. Collecting stamps were becoming particularly annoying. Many people enjoy this part of the Camino and end up keeping their passports as treasured keepsakes. With my minimalist mindset, I imagined it would not stay with me long after I had received my Compestella. A necessary evil I suppose.



Anit Climax. That’s how I would express the Compestella. Covered in scaffolding pictures were not really exciting. Even receiving my certificate didn’t offer me much satisfaction. By this point, the journey had given me so much more than either of the two former could ever offer. It’s funny that we pass by so much beauty in quest of this church, almost ironic.

I still wanted a decent conclusion and so I  decided to head to Cape Finisterre. Many pilgrims continue there journey onto this point. It also holds the official o.oo km marker stone. I managed to get there just after sunset completing the 90 km in one day. It was great to be back near the ocean and the way there was a beautiful cycle.



To celebrate I spent three nights in a flash hotel. This was a milestone I didn’t want to go unnoticed. While to some this may have just been the end of a long trip. To me, this was my rebirth. From here on out I was a new person. I would dedicate myself to being the absolute best I could be. Every day until death.

I held a ceremony on a rock overhanging the ocean. Here I said some words and forgave myself for any of my undesirable past actions. I spoke a little about my past, and I made some pledges for my future. I announced loudly that I was now forgiven and reborn before washing myself in a deep rock pool. No one was around but the birds and the waves. This ceremony was for me, and me alone. I threw my burdens into the ocean and I hiked back up the cliff a lighter happier me.

Looking back at why I left London and started this cycle. I received what I was after and so much more!

## Day 69 – 79
Now began the next leg of the trip. It was strange as my motives had now changed dramatically. I no longer wanted to live off the land, nor was I as excited about camping. Preferring to take the quicker routes than the quieter ones and reaching Lisbon was now my priority.

I mentally categorised this leg as the strengthening of my foundations. My focus now on building healthy habits and supportive thinking. I feel as though I have a sense of direction and purpose. At times it feels as though these final few kilometres are holding me back from accomplishing the things I want to achieve next. However, I am committed to completing the route to Lisbon. I made a promise to myself, and that I will never break.



I feel happy to spend the night in hostels and this has allowed me to meet some new interesting characters. Taking the time to understand them and their stories is helping me strengthen my own beliefs. We all walk such different paths, what is a weakness to one is a strength to another. When you stop judging yourself by other peoples success and failures it becomes easier to truly listen.



The road continues to throw up its challenges. I have faced a tire that keeps slipping off, a destroyed front hub, and I’m currently still sourcing a replacement rear wheel. It seems that the weight of my bags became too much for Zen and the damage to her rear wheel is irreparable. Ironically this is one of the most costly repairs I have needed to make and it comes 3 days before the finish line. But get there we must, and get there we will.

Surprisingly I feel little hesitation about arriving in Lisbon. Instead, I am excited to complete this stage and move onto my next one. While I will definitely be touring again, I now know undeniably that I want to keep some of my creature comforts. A walk on the wild side is a romantic getaway, but I am not willing to sacrifice modern luxuries indefinitely. I will, however, continue to live a more modest lifestyle.

So as soon as I am able to replace my rear wheel, which is currently on order at the local bike shop. I will be back on the road for my final three days of cycling before reaching Lisbon. After which I intend to hold some unforgettable celebrations. Another milestone and a heck of an achievement. Six months ago I could never have imagined myself completing something like this.

Whats after Lisbon? The intention is to sail to the Carribean. I will be working on the specifics once I’m a little more settled. The thought of a transatlantic crossing excites me and I think it is the next worthy goal.
