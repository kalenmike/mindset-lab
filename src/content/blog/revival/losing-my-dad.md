Losing my dad was the hardest experience of my life. It was like my body and mind glitched. I couldn't quite understand what was happening inside me. I teleported from one feeling to another. I wanted to join him; I wanted to live. I wanted to become a success; I wanted to sleep all day. I felt the need to avenge him; I felt the need to let him go in peace. Anger, love, hate, fear, gratitude, shame. I felt them all and more.

## The Last Message

My dad sent me a message on Saturday morning. I replied briskly. It was a difficult topic to get into so early in the morning and was better served as a call rather than a text. I would be calling him the next day, and we typically spoke for a few hours at a time.

I had been battling depression for a few weeks, and my spirits were already low. I was trying to support my girlfriend, who had suddenly lost one of her best friends to cancer when doctors suddenly gave him 2 weeks to live and began declining immediately.

In short, I was already running on empty.

## In a Blink of an Eye

My phone rang early Sunday morning; it was around 5 am. I answered; one of my dad's employees forced out, "Tim's dead".

My dad had crashed his quad bike on the way home. He bled out on the street for 4 hours before help came. Passersby watching as the police prevented anyone from helping him. He was already gone when the ambulance finally arrived.

## Grief Driven Action

Initially, I felt responsible for letting everyone else know, and spent the first hour calling my family who were spread across the world. Then I spent the rest of the day in shock. My brain could not understand the news. It was looking for a way to fix it all, to undo it all.

On Monday, I left on my journey to take care of everything and get some answers. The worst moment of my life, and I was completely alone. But I felt I needed to do this. My dad would have done the same for me.


## The Aftermath

Things got really crazy. Conspiracy opened up as more information arrived. The police wanted me to bribe them to get more information. People came out of the woodwork trying to get their hands on anything and everything they could. In my worst moment, I experienced the worst of humanity.

 I'll save the rest of those chaotic details for another time. Today, I feel like focusing purely on the grief.

When I eventually got back home, I was fatherless. My sister had also chosen to cut off contact. My family had shrunk by 2 in less than 8 days. It's always taken me a long time to truly let people in, so my core network has been my family. With one or two really trusted friends, and my partner. This core network of 5 dropped to 3 almost overnight.


## How Priorities Change

My other connections didn't feel so important. My dad's death acted like a filter that showed me more clearly where I was valued and who really mattered.

If it wasn't for my girlfriend, I'm sure I would have spent much of the initial months alone. People don't know how to handle this kind of grief; they don't have the words, and they don't want to think about losing their own parents. So they offer their condolences and then quietly slip out the back. They offer to be there, knowing you won't ask them to be. Then go on with their lives.

## Walking Alone

There is something about coming to peace with knowing you are alone in your suffering. That nobody can really be there for you in any meaningful way. You have to do this alone, even if you are lucky enough to have people next to you.

Losing a parent changes you. You die as well, then you are reborn as someone else. A little wiser, a little more empathetic, a little more aware of finality. You begin to understand that most things in life are not worth your time. That other people's opinions will never mean as much as the one you lost. That you are more alone now than you have ever been. Instead of breaking you, it fortifies you inside. You become more self-sufficient. You want to give more than you want to take. And you are happy to walk away from those who seem to be on a different wavelength.

## The Rebirth

Losing my dad broke me into a thousand pieces, and as I lay there something started putting me back together. But I was rebuilt as someone different. I have the memories of who I once was, but I can never be that person again. I am now someone new.
