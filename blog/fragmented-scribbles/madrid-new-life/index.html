Madrid, The New Life

Waking up to chirping birds has become a regular occurrence. Occasionally the clashing of a glass in the distance meets the strum of a Spanish guitar, signalling the start of the day. It is always hot and for that my window remains open. I’m reminded that this is Spain. Often I’m filled with a sense of pride when I think back on the three weeks I’ve been here.

Despite having traveled and even relocated many times, this is the first time I’ve attempted to settle somewhere where I cannot rely on my English. The 5 months I spent traveling in Spain in 2012 gives me an obvious advantage. Even though my grammar needs some serious work I am able to communicate and sometimes forget I’m not speaking my mother tongue.

I find Madrid to be a fascinating city and I am particularly enamored with Retiro park. Drawn there on a daily basis to join in the athletic displays on the local calisthenic bars. Between sets, I find it exhilarating to watch the locals skateboard, roller-blade and even cycle backwards around the fountain in front of me. The years I spent in London conditioned me to have a certain opinion of cities, an opinion which is quickly being shattered by the Spanish capital. Rather than the cosmopolitan display of suits and high fashion I find this city filled with trendy youth covered in tattoos and sporting interesting and unusual hairstyles. Perhaps I fit in here, walking around, Spotify blasting away on my headphones, it does feel like home.

In the weeks leading up to today, I managed to secure work, a place to live and navigate the confusing Spanish bureaucracy to obtain temporary legal status. I have been blessed to meet many amazing people. While many often move on to Barcelona, San Sebastián or even back towards their homes, I am truly grateful to have shared in their lives for just a moment.

Rohan, the friendly Indian who welcomed me on my first day in Madrid, embarked on his journey to share with his mother that he had been living a double life and was in-fact gay. He confided in me the irrational fear that arises from living in a country that does not allow people to be themselves. When he messaged me to say he was leaving Spain and his mother took the news well, I felt my heart skip a beat. For that short moment, I could share in his happiness. He could now be himself, if nowhere else at least at home.

Izabela emancipated herself at 16, not because of family problems but because she craved independence and that paired with her intelligence enabled her to start university at that young age. Despite being only 21 as she sits across the table from me, I sense an old soul. With wisdom and compassion, she quotes many masters and shares her experiences traveling the world. She provides me with faith, faith that humanity can do better, that we can be better.

These are only two of the awe-inspiring characters I have met. Each unique individual inspiring me in some small way to be grateful for what I have, to fight for what I want and to never give up. This beautiful city, these wonderful people, they give me pride in my existence and I remain humble in this honour. How I wish I could share the blessings of this life with everyone I hold dear. For now, know that I hold you all in my heart with me.
