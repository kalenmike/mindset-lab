Blind Amnesia

The more time that passes, the more the good gets highlighted, and the bad fades into nothingness.

I’m missing my ex today. It’s been almost exactly 2 months, and I guess things hit harder on milestones. The more time that passes, the more the good gets highlighted, and the bad fades into nothingness.

It was my choice to walk away. Our arguments were becoming more frequent, and instead of seeing growth in how we resolved things, I saw decline. I became increasingly frustrated at how long it took for the smallest issues to get resolved. It was in that frustration that I began to realise I was becoming someone I did not want to be.

Dealing with the passing of my dad had a big effect on it all. I was letting myself go. I stopped exercise and I wasn’t eating much. But I couldn’t manage to pull myself together because every time I found the smallest amount of motivation or happiness an argument would erupt and pull me back down.

I knew that I had to walk away, but I still loved her. It’s a cruel thing, love, in many ways. How we love what is not good for us. How we spend so much time hoping that things will improve despite all evidence to the contrary.

I finally found the courage to leave. And life did get better. For me, it almost always does after a breakup. I tend to use the time to focus inward, to find myself again, and to return to who I am. And that’s exactly what happened this time. I got back to being me.

Without the regular arguments, my free time was filled with peace and silence. All year I had needed that, and finally it was here. There was now a space for me to feel and to begin truly healing the wounds that my dad’s passing had left behind.

Still, I long for the good times. The happiness in imagining a future together. The warm cuddles and comforting laughs. No relationship is all bad, and walking away leaves you missing what had been good all along.

I catch myself in moments as I notice the blind amnesia. The focus on only what was good. I forget that I had lost myself; I forget that I had been lacking the room to breathe, and I forget that I was not able to show up authentically in my grief.

I’m having one of those moments right now as I write this. Looking back on old photos, following old chains of thought. Questioning my decisions. There is a sadness, but behind it, logic still rules. I did what was best for me. At least for now.
