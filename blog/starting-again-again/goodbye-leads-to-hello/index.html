Goodbye Always Leads to Hello

Except for the two airport personnel, security was empty. Not something I usually experienced on international flights. After the usual undress and pat down I found myself in the departure terminal. Smaller than usual it was just what you would expect from such a small island. This was it I was one step closer to leaving the place I called home for the last 3 months.

I identified the gate and put on some music. The intention was to drown out the thinking that inevitably occurs when you leave loved ones behind. But even distractions can only hold you for so long. The flight was delayed for unknown reasons and I eventually grew tired of the pacing and head bobbing. I retired to a chair. We finally began the boarding process and I found the music no longer offered me solace.

As the longing and reminiscing began it occurred to me that I had not yet left the island. Sitting in the plane I could see the small hills and fences that separated those leaving from those staying. Only a mere 10 minutes drive away was everything I now missed. Take off took us over the roads I had been driving on and then over the areas I had worked and played. I held back the tears by reminding myself to look forward and not back. I could not undo the past but if the absence of this place, of these people, became unbearable I could always return.

This island, this beautiful blue sea. I have been blessed to spend so much time in such an awe inspiring place. Surely I would find myself here again. As the memories flooded my mind I found myself battling the fear of the unknown, and perhaps the fear of missing out. This was it, this was life. I’m sure one day I would look back at this very moment and find it to be an interesting story. Maybe it would teach me some life changing lessons or maybe I would just laugh at my naivety. For surely this was just a temporary trip away, like leaving home to buy bread only to return with more than you had when you left.

My time on paradise had taught me much. My character flaws had been pointed out and carved into my mind were paths towards improvement. Unbelievable characters had introduced themselves. At times I felt I was living in a novella. A small story about the island life and how you can choose to turn any situation into a gift or a nightmare.

If grateful was a strong enough word I would use it. Yet it is found lacking in my appreciation for what has come my way in the recent months. And I only hope that I was worthy to receive such blessings. So out into the world I fly, crossing oceans, changing time zones and experiencing new cultures. In my heart, believing that once again those wonderful characters will make an appearance. And hopefully when they do, I will be able to give them back what they have given me, and then some.
