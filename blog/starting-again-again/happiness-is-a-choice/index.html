Happiness is a Choice

Spanish music plays silently in the background as I gaze out of the third floor window. Desert plants decorate the window sill and bring a sense of comfort. The room is painted in an Autumn style. Almost ironic as I look out into Summer.

In a few days I will have been in this bustling city for three weeks. The first week felt like it took a month to pass yet the following two passed as quickly as a meteor in the night sky. The rich culture and foreign language captivated the senses.

It was easy to be at peace in this moment, I had come far since arriving in Spain. With a backpack, €600, and a credit card already €800 in debt I started my adventure. I always knew that I was spending more than I had. Throughout my life I have not allowed myself to get into debt with financial institutions, opting rather to pay off any borrowed money before the interest set in. Yet I had been practicing daily to let go of these thoughts. To remain at peace despite my worries.

This time around it was not a luxury I could afford. I needed the cash to secure a rented room. And I would need to keep using the credit card until such time as my incomings surpassed my outgoings.

I suppose I could have been more careful to start. Perhaps I ate out more often than I needed to. It was my first hostel accommodation that made budgeting difficult. The hostel was clean, safe, and warm. It offered most of what I needed. In fact it was this hostel that helped me to secure my first job, taking travelers out to eat Tapas. A traditional Spanish snack that is usually served alongside alcohol. The challenge at the time was the missing kitchen. Without a kitchen most of my meals were from restaurants. The vibrant vegan community in Madrid meant that I was not short of options. Having spent 3 months on a small island with little change, the diversity had my mouth watering.

When I eventually moved myself into a cheaper hostel and one that included a kitchen. I found that I could spend €4 for 5 days. This made a huge impact on my budgeting. I always remind myself not to look back, yet it’s hard not to see the savings I could have made from moving here first. Everything happens for a reason.

The difficulty of hostel life is that you find yourself never alone. 24 hours 7 days a week you have people around you. Your isolation mostly includes time in the bathroom. Even then there is always noise reminding you that you are not alone. The smells make sleeping interesting and there is always someone coming or going. Your sleep is never solid and you end up waking up several times throughout the night, every night.

Yet despite these challenges, it is humbling to have little more than a rented bed and a locker. Part unpacking and packing everyday. The truth of it is that in comparison to my 3 months cycling Western Europe this was luxury. Even the cold shower was better than no shower at all. I often find myself amazed at how little we really need. Once we lay down roots we begin to accumulate so many things and we truly believe we need them all. When you wake up with nothing more than what you can carry you feel truly free and you begin to question the origin of your thoughts before.

After three weeks I find myself here, finally alone. I managed to secure a rented room and with my part time work I am only making a loss of €65 per week.

I never really find myself feeling at home. At least in the traditional sense of settling down. I have however always loved Spain and the opportunity to pass some time here truly excites me.

The song on the radio changes and I’m back here in the present. One deep breathe follows the next. Happiness is truly a choice.
