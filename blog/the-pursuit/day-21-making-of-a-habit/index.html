Day Twenty-One: Wild Boar Encounters and the Three-Week Habit

Staring down a dozen glowing eyes in the dark, snapping a chain tool, and realizing the wild road is finally starting to feel like home.

“The night is long and full of terrors”, echoed in my head as I found myself hiding behind a tree frantically searching for anything I could climb. The roar came again, louder this time. I was too frightened to turn on my light and attract attention, I just couldn’t tell what creature I was facing…

It’s a funny thing our fear of the dark and of the unknown. It makes sense from an evolutionary perspective but we sometimes struggle to correlate this to the new world humans have created.

When I last paused a moment to share my adventures it was just approaching Christmas and the holiday spirit had me down. Thank you to everyone who got in touch and wished me well and added to the magic that is this time of year. I was especially lucky to be put up in a chateau for one night. The place was absolutely beautiful and I took advantage of the access to a hot bath and a heated swimming pool. I truly recommend Chateau de Jallanges to anyone who needs to get away from it all and rest within themselves. From reading the notes left behind it seems to also make a great honeymoon destination.

I was up early the morning of day 20, well rested and feeling as though I had a sense of inner peace. The luxuries had me feeling like royalty and I agonised over the thought of enduring the open road, in what seemed like more rain. These extreme changes in situations are troubling to deal with, yet it is this exact spectrum that opens us up to learn to appreciate the things and situations we often take for granted.

After breakfast it was time to burn rubber and I made my way further South. So far on my trip I have found it the most difficult to camp out when I am in the larger towns and cities. I haven’t quite mastered using the locals to my advantage as explained here and I also prefer the solitude of camping wild in an isolated spot, at least when this is possible. Being close to Tours, a large city, I needed to cover some ground if I intended to camp as I usually did.

The roads began to get quieter and the landscape changed from blocks of flats with peeling paint to large green trees and open fields. I could see the vast empty road in front of me and as I turned my head the triumph of civilisation behind. Just then a dog charged towards me, barking, as they often like to do when passing by houses. The bark sounded more friendly than anything and it that moment I was overcome by my emotions.

Tears formed in my eyes as I felt compassion for this animal who I imagined was wishing me well on my pursuit. I thought of all the pleasures I had enjoyed these last three days and pondered how I would make this way of life sustainable. It’s the fear of going back to what my life used to be, coupled with the longing for the luxuries it offered. Watching Netflix in the comfort of a soft bed, tasty food at the ready, meeting friends for a coffee, these things I still wanted. Unfortunately so far in my experience they had come with working long hours most of the week, being rudely awakened each morning by an electronic slave driver, and crammed commutes with grumpy faces. These things, on the other hand, I wanted to avoid like the plague. How do you have your cake and eat it?!

All in all this was a happy day and the weather cleared up as the time past. I found a camp spot in a very isolated woodland and began sketching the plants around me. I decided the only way to get better at foraging for food was to learn what I was looking at and this hobby would fit in well with the mycology I was practising.

After setting up camp and constructing a DIY bike stand from a split tree I made some much-needed adjustments to my gears and fully serviced my rear derailer. I did this by stripping it down and greasing it before putting it back together. This seemed to solve most of the problems I was having. It was distracting working on Zen and the time flew by as I toiled behind my head lamp. The total isolation of this particular camp spot caused no concern for the light pollution.

I froze as I began hearing loud growl like noises. I couldn’t identify the noise and I had no idea what I was up against. I waited it out in complete silence until I noticed the rummaging and growling was getting closer. I took a few steps towards the noise and it got worse. At this point I felt extremely vulnerable. Was this a bear? A wolf? Some other type of biting, slashing, sprinting animal?

The trees had no low hanging branches which were strong enough to support my weight, both my knifes were under 3 inches long and my food was scattered around my campsite. The time for action was now but not knowing what to do I stood frozen for a moment, half hidden behind a tree. The noises got closer. The choice was to either abandon the site along with my gear and hope for the best or attempt to frighten off whatever this was. I opted for the latter. I put my head lamp on the brightest setting and moved it frantically in the direction of the noises.

The first thing I saw was eye’s lit up everywhere, at least 12 pairs. My heart stopped. I giggled, these were only wild boar. I was aware of the damage they could cause but I also knew they would do me no harm if I let them be. I turned around and headed back to my tent startling them away as I moved. Crisis averted, it was time to lay back and count the sheep.

The trees had a golden shimmer as I peered out my tent in the morning. I did a mental fist pump as I noticed the blue skies and lack of clouds. Everything was bone dry, hallelujah! On the road I appreciated the quick reaction of my gears, the work from the night before had been a success. Passing by a truly super, super market, I resupplied and must have been overzealous because it ended up taking me about an hour to get everything back on Zen. In the end I had to eat a good lunch to make the room.

A strong wind came against me dropping my speed to under 10km/h as I rode on long flat stretches. I began feeling a small vibration when pedalling and later identified that my chain had broken free at one link, not enough to snap but enough to cause some slight issues. The outer link must have gotten bent from riding like this as I ended up snapping my chain tool trying to fix it. Great! I now needed a new chain tool and it seemed like a new chain.

I had purchased and fitted this chain myself before setting off on the journey and since then I had learned that the speed of the chain relates only to the rear sprockets and not the whole drive train. This meant my chain was about 1 mm too thin. This explained some of my shifting troubles.

Camp was next to a river facing East which offered a beautiful sunrise in the morning. The plan for the day, get to a bike shop and replace Zen’s chain. Hopefully without the chain snapping entirely or causing damage to anything else. I am happy to say this mission was a success.

So here I am, fresh chain, new tool and ready to set out on the road again. I have heard it said that it takes 3 weeks to make or break a habit. If there is any truth to that then this way of life has now become a habit and I can expect things to get a whole lot easier. I really want to start eating more from the land and finding better ways to collect water and I would appreciate any tips.

The road is long and winding, may the call of the wild comfort you. Until next time, Safaman signing out.
