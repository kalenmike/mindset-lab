Day Forty-Four: Finding the Camino and My Soul on the Road to Santiago

Swapping inland roads for the coastal Camino de Santiago, catching up with an old friend in the Basque country, and enduring the hardest cycling yet.

It’s impossible to express what has happened since I last shared my journey with you. These have been some of the most trying days for me and I feel I have done some tremendous growing emotionally. The solitude of the journey has begun to show cracks in my armour and the hardest cycling yet has me questioning the way forward…

The beautiful landscape and views of the Basque country come with some of the steepest hills I have faced yet and I am finding myself walking more hills than cycling. Some of the journey has taken me off road onto clay which sucks up my tires like a pebble dropped into water. My Converse boots while waterproof aren’t made for hiking and grip is becoming a problem. It often takes me an extremely long time to climb hills as I literally inch the wheels forward and stop to prevent myself slipping back down. Without my new brake pads this route may have not been possible.

After leaving McDonalds on day 35 locating a campsite proved difficult. There just wasn’t anywhere suitable to set up and no hostels or hotels where available. It was dark, it was cold and my head buzzed from all the coffee I had been drinking. My frustration increased as kilometre after kilometre no suitable camp spots showed up.

It got to be 2 am and I was starting to doze off, things were seeming pretty miserable and then my front tire went flat. “Are you f**king kidding me” I thought as I did my best to hold my tongue. I pushed Zen for awhile hoping that a camp spot was around the corner before caving and removing my tire to patch the leak. I stopped just under one of the only street lights in site and just managed to unpack my tools before disturbing a local dog who barked non stop waking all the other dogs in the area. I didn’t know who to blame but I wanted to blame someone, I managed to remind myself that no one was conspiring against me. Woo Saa!

After being unable to find the source of the puncture I thought maybe the valve had leaked and put everything back together and set off. Unfortunately I was wrong and the tire went flat about 50 metres down the road. No light in site, full of frustration and half asleep I settle for pushing the bike.

After giving up hope I eventually camped just off the road on what must have been someone’s driveway. I raised my tent but didn’t unpack anything except my sleeping bag and left very early the next morning. It proved to be a very cold nights sleep and this coupled with the short hours meant I was nothing but grumpy for most of the day. Although I did remind myself at regular intervals that everything would annoy me on the day and that I should try and accept this fact.

It rained all day but I managed to cover a good amount of kilometres. Eventually I passed a window sign that showed rooms were available and I decided to check in. A good nights sleep was needed. It wasere that I decided, with the help of some advisers, that I would change my course. Instead of heading directly to Lisbon I was going to join the Camino de Santiago and go via Santiago de Compestela. This is something that I had wanted to do for a long time since hearing about it some years ago and being only about 70 kilometres away from the route I figured why not. It also meant that my journey would be mostly coastal instead of inland which would be a little warmer.

After eating and cleaning my gear I passed out and slept till late the next morning before heading back out just in time for more rain. I didn’t manage to cycle very far before I passed by what appeared to be a very good camping spot. The poor experiences I had so far camping in Spain made this opportunity unmissable and I decided it was worth stopping rather than risking more bad luck. Camp was perfect and despite the constant rain I remained warm and dry and my confidence improved as I began to believe I would manage in Spain after all. I slept on a slope which meant I was sliding down all night but hey this was better than a driveway and at least I was hidden.

Day 38 was hard work and I faced mostly hills. It hard to express the despair that confronts you when you reach the top of a hill only to see another even longer one ahead. The difficulty was increasing and it was taking most of my mental strength to keep pushing forward. I wasn’t really sure what I was doing and after questioning myself I found strength in my new purpose to reach the Compestela.

When the road flattened out a little I crossed an adequate campsite, close to the road but well hidden, so I prepared for the night. The next day I was within reach of an old friends house which also happened to be on the Camino. We made arrangements to meet and my sole purpose of the day was to get there. Facing more trying hills I eventually made it. I was now on the Camino and also with a friend. Happiness was again inside me.

It was a really great experience to spend time in Larrabetzu with my friend. A very open minded guy it was easy talk about the more spiritual side of life and as we are both vegan we shared many similar views. I ended up spending four days there, much more than expected. These days gave me enough time to fully service my bike, pick up some supplies, full up with some great vegan food and enjoy company.

When I eventually left I was on a mission to find a credencial or pilgrims passport. This is a document you use to collect stamps from various locations along the Camino. When you reach the Compestela they then use this document to prove that you actually took the route. I was unable to find one and as it was late so I decided to check into a hostel nearby hoping to get some information. This paid off and I picked one up the same night. Being in a hostel also meant I got to meet some fellow travellers and hear about similar journeys which was a welcomed treat.

It’s now time to get back on the road and head towards the ocean, after my longest time in civilisation so far the open road seems scary again. I am now dealing with some heavy emotions as the road begins to take its toll. I go forward with all my might as I accept this challenge for what it is and I hope to use the Camino to cleanse my soul and come out the other side a better person.
