Day Fifty Eight - Battling Those Internal Daemons

These last few days have served to prove some theory’s I have been creating. I’ve found myself on top of the world only to be kicked back under the table. Is it possible to have only good things in your life? Or is it perhaps the things we perceive as bad that help to create the good moments? Without a comparison can something be either one way or the other? I believe that they go hand in hand and if we want to soar with the eagles we need to learn to appreciate diving with the fish.

Day 56

I woke up early to get some surfing in, it was cold and the sun was hiding but we were leaving today and I had to have one last ride. I wasn’t ready to leave and join the ‘camino’, it now seemed a distant memory. In its place I found Salinas, it’s sunshine and the beach. For now they were making me happy and there was no need to move on. Like a dream this was a tiny isolated world that contained everything I needed. A warm home with modern luxuries, the ocean, vegan junk food and best of all sunny days. The open road in comparison was cold, unknown and difficult. This was one of the first times I genuinely did not want to continue my pursuit.

The surf didn’t help to calm my mind as I found the ocean rough and the current strong. I managed a few waves before leaving the waters and heading back to the hostel. I had some work to get done on my website and I wanted to finish it off before I left. Leaving things unfinished is giving me anxiety. Surrounded by such beauty and calmness I see now that I am the source of my anxiety. When there is nothing around to blame you often have to face the hard truth.

Noon was approaching, ‘check out time’, I thought. A broken spoke on my rear wheel still needed to be replaced before I could leave. Stress came over me and rather than accept this panic and rush I decided it was worth spending an extra day to sort myself out. I called up the hostel owner and extended by one night.

Besides stepping out for an hour to have a quick lunch I spent the whole day and evening working online. I researched some questions I had been listing, updated my website and watched some online guru’s explain their business on youtube. The day flew by and before I knew it I was looking at the clock to find 2 am staring back at me. Unhappily I called it a night and left my work space to be sorted out the next morning. This was the beauty of hostel living in the winter, instead of just 1 bed I had the whole house to myself.

Day 57

The day started off good as I packed up my gear and got ready to leave. I was slow, probably too slow. When you spend so many days on the road even two days indoors feels much longer. When you extend those two days to a week one can barely even remember what it was like out there. Granted I had travelled in the last 6 days but I also hadn’t camped and that really does change the dynamic.

The day was hot, the skies blue and I knew to make an effort to enjoy it. The weather forecast was showing rain for the next 10 days. This was the most miserable forecast I have had yet. Even France showed some partly cloudy days in the 10 day forecast! It’s not the rain so much that bothers me. My gear is pretty waterproof and I don’t really get that wet anymore. Rather it’s just that everything seems to take longer in the rain, the ride seems more like a chore than something to enjoy and mildew starts to grow whatever I fail to dry properly.

Ah, mildew! I hate the stuff, I’ve lived with asthma since I was a young child and there are only really three things that give me trouble, with mildew being the worst. I recently found some inside my tent, now its growing inside my inflatable camping mat. From experience it seems much easier to replace rather than battle to salvage items infected with this survival expert.

I was unable to replace my broken spoke after all as I couldn’t get the freewheel off. So I loaded up and went in search of something that would do the trick. After stopping at a few shops and struggling to explain what I needed I eventually bought a large shifting spanner. I put off doing the repair and cycled further before another spoke popped and I decided it couldn’t be put off any longer.

While removing the freewheel I noticed the bearings were in a bad way and I opened up the hub for a service. A clean and some new grease was all I needed but I replaced the bearings anyway. Putting everything back together I started setting the wheel true before realising that my spoke tool was too big for the nipples. A little fiddling managed to get Zen to ride while rubbing the left chain stay once every revolution.

Frustrated it was a good thing I was alone, it took effort to remind myself to stay calm and take it easy. I rode on a short distance with a the wheel looking like I was just in an accident before I set up camp in a forest nearby. Just in time it turns out as it started raining once the tarp was up.

I didn’t do much that evening as it was freezing cold, I wasn’t in the best mood and after eating some dinner I thought it was time for bed. In a matter of two days I went from on top of the world to feeling like everything was just not worth it. This realisation made me smile.

Day 58

The alarm went off at it’s usual time, 7 am is just to early for me these days. Initially the point of this alarm was to have me packed up and ready to leave my camp spots before anyone else was awake. Mostly I find myself snoozing or turning the alarm off these days. I must be over confident as I no longer fear someone discovering me. The strange thing is that I tend not to sleep through the night and my sleep is broken into two periods. This means I get some reading or thinking done between 1 and 3 am before sleeping again till around 9 am.

It was really cold outside and my sleeping bag was warm so I stayed in bed longer than I would normally. I was under a tarp in my hammock which meant I had a view of outside without needing to open anything up. This is one of my favourite things about not camping in a tent. When I wake up in a tent I have no idea what is going on outside until I unzip it all and by that time I’m up and moving. Hammock camping on the other hand allows me to watch the world go by while I do some reflecting without needing to leave the warmth of my cocoon.

Camping was strange and I had some bad dreams, the few days I had spent indoors made this all new again. I was slower at everything as my routine had been broken but I managed to get breakfast cooked and to handle nature’s call. I packed up my gear and got back on the road, despite the rain the general feeling was positive.

Squeek, Squeek went the rear mudguard. Somehow it was now rubbing my wonky wheel and I couldn’t manage to stop it from slowing me down. I ripped it off, carefully. The map showed the nearest bike shop about 80 km away as the crow flies. This was probably around the 160 km if you take into account all the turns from mountain roads. No way I could make it. I was going to be on this banged up wheel for a few days at my current pace!

My headset had also come loose and as I wasn’t carrying a 34 mm spanner I was going to have to ride slowly. With the wonky rear wheel and shaky front wheel stability was non existent. The ride was mostly rural and I never went through any towns that would offer me the required tools so even a friendly neighbour wasn’t an option.

Every cloud has it’s silver lining and I got to enjoy some really beautiful ocean views. I was high up most of the day so there was almost always a view and my slow pace meant there wasn’t much else to do but to enjoy it. I kept riding until darkness was approaching and then I veered into a small forest that dipped off a cliff and set up camp. Within minutes it went from dry to hailing and I knew I was in for a cold night.

There are so many levels of emotions that I am dealing with on this trip and every time I feel I have passed through I find there was something hiding underneath. Life is such a beautiful thing that I took for granted much more than I would like to admit. My worst days and my best days all tell my story and I am so happy to have a story to tell.
