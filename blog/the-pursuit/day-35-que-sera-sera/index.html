Day Thirty-Five: Wiping Out on Wet Leaves and Entering Spain

Recovering from a brutal crash in France, replacing a shattered luggage rack, and stumbling upon a starry beach valley on the border.

It came out of nowhere. There was a sudden pull to the right and I lost control, the rear wheel slid. Every attempt to correct the impeeding disaster proved unsuccessful. I was going down, Zen was going down and we both knew it. Like a cowardly captain I abandoned ship and fate took its control.

I took my time preparing to leave Bordeaux on day 30 my gear was scattered around my friends house and I didn’t want to forget anything vital behind. We had breakfast and lunch together before I set off back into the unknown. The two days I had spent in civilisation felt much longer, it was a strange thing being around people I could talk too after so long and having access to so many shops had me tempted to splurge. I held back and only bought what I needed remembering that space and weight was limited. I did get to enjoy a vegan burger, something I had been craving. I still love my junk food!

The weather was manageable, there was a light rain but that was expected. Zen was fully tuned up and riding smoother than ever. The route was mostly cycle lanes which meant easy riding. Being so caught up in the moment I didn’t get far before hitting some wet leaves and taking a dive. Bang!

Zen’s handle bar tape was shredded, and so was the left pannier bag. My waterproof bag! The crutch of my whole journey, the only thing I had full faith in. I was gutted, my head exploded with judgements. If only I could go back and undo this mistake but alas this ain’t no video game and there ain’t no load previous save. This was going to be the way forward.

A concerned resident came out to help but I assured him I was going to live and once he realised I couldn’t speak French he headed back inside. After getting up and analysing the damage I pulled out the duct and insulation tape and made quick work of the holes and torn handlebar tape. “How long would this stay water tight”, was the thought that kept ringing in my head.

The fall had done more damage that was harder to see initially which I would discover later. The first of which was my neatly tuned gears were now out of wack and my brakes were unbalanced causing the rear wheel to stay stationary when moving. Annoyed, and desperately wanting to get back on the road I did what I could to get moving. I punished myself most of the way before I eventually accepted the reality of what had happened or tried really, really hard to.

Camp was the best I have had so far with a full tarp and chair. So good in fact I decided to stay put for the whole next day. It seemed like I was at a rented campsite. I spent the day making net from some rope, cooking up foraged foods and attempting to make extra rope from plant fibre. Time flew by much more than I would have expected and it was a great day outdoors.

Back on the road for day 32 it rained constantly. The ride was flat most of the way and it is included in one of the easiest stretches I have done to date. I reflected on acceptance most of the ride until I was stopped abruptly by the rear wheel seizing. It took me a moment to realise that the pannier rack had now completely snapped in two places leaving only one of eight welds remaining. Ok, the honest truth is I heard a problem for about 25 kilometres before this and refusing to face reality I kept riding. I might have been able to do a better job salvaging the rack if I had stopped sooner but none-the-less my now dear friend duct tape got us roadworthy.

I kept riding until I hit the Atlantic, it was a long, flat and wet ride. I was kept going by the thought of seeing the beach. It was worth it as I was rewarded with the most beautiful sunset. I took a moment to truly take in what I was seeing and I think it was at this point that I finally understood, “what will be, will be”. Being extremely grateful I didn’t mind the time passing and found a place to camp just before midnight.

I was close to the ocean, but still in a forest. This meant I was hidden and got to enjoy the sounds but not the sights. I couldn’t see much anyway as it was pitch black with the clouds blocking the stars and moon.

The next day was mostly uneventful but it was dry and this is to be cherished! I enjoyed breakfast on the beach and then travelled on mostly flat terrain along a cycle path before eventually camping in another forest not far from the beach. This was proving to be an easy ride so far and I was hoping it would stay this way as I approached Spain.

Up early the morning of day 34 my goal was to get to a cycle store for a replacement pannier rack. The thought of my rack breaking on a hill at 50 km/h plagued my mind. I passed some very beautiful towns on the way stopping for lunch. You could tell these places thrived in summer and I imagine that this particular stretch of beach front makes an exceptional summer holiday destination.

My entire afternoon was spent repairing, replacing and upgrading parts on Zen. I was in a tank top just outside the store I had purchase the parts from enjoying the 23 degree weather. The sun set and once again I was left searching for a place to camp in the dark. It quickly got late after I stopped at a supermarket on the way for a top up of my groceries. I may have been stressed about this if I hadn’t now learnt to fully trust the way forward.

If there is one thing that has rung out on this journey it is that everything will always work out in the end. Que sera, sera! Take that thought and reflect on it. You will then see the true serenity that it holds.

My aimless riding got me lost in the most beautiful of places as I found an isolated valley right on the beach. The perfect camping spot. The stars were out in full force and the skies were clear. I vainly attempted to photograph the utter beauty of this moment for you, but alas iPhones just aren’t made for night photography and all I got was a black square.

Several shooting stars passed overhead as the waves crashed on the shore in front of me. To the far left I could see the occasional blink of a light house as it did its dues. It’s impossible for me to express how thankful I was to be able to participate in such magic. Despite all the chaos we as humans create, the world is still beautiful and life still carries on as it has for millenia. If you so wished you could return to the ways of your forefathers. This thought brought me peace.

I’ve now arrived in Spain for the second leg of this journey and have to run as McDonalds is kicking me out and its time to find a home for the night. Hasta luego!
