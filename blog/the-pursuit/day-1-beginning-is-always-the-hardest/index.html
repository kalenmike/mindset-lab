Day 1: 130 Kilometers, Broken Gear, and Surviving Our First Day on Bikes

A brutal 130-kilometer awakening, mechanical failures, and navigating out of London on the very first day of our cycle tour to Lisbon.

Early Mornings

A sudden jerk shifted me out of my dream state. My subconsciousness’s attempt to incorporate the melodic beeping into my dream had failed. This signals the start of the beginning. Reality doesn’t manage to set in until you are brushing the sleep from your eyes much earlier than you normally would.

One hundred and thirty kilometres! That is what we are facing today. I think know I haven’t even cycled that far in the last three years combined.

As is often the case it was difficult getting myself prepared in the morning (I love a sleep in). Something in the back of my mind tempted me to climb back into bed and forget about the whole thing. I pushed it back into the depths it belongs. I often find it strange how sleep seems to solve any problem (until you wake up). But there was no turning back now though, the proverbial ship had sailed.

We loaded up the bicycles, hereafter referred to as Zen and Dave. Zen would be my noble steed, after her model name Zenith. And Dave was, well Dave. Breakfast was quick and rushed. My main priority was ensuring I left the house in a good condition. I was counting on receiving the deposit to help fund my journey. The last thing I wanted was a deduction for something I could easily have rectified.

Escaping London

Despite our most optimistic intentions to leave the house by 5:30 am we eventually set off around 7 am. The air was chilly and it felt fresh as it entered my lungs. An odd statement to make in a big city like London, and I suppose this was going to be an odd day.

It took both us some time to get used to the weight of the bikes. It’s not every day you ride around with 30 kilograms of extra weight. We took special care in ensuring that the weight was evenly distributed on the left and the right. This didn’t make adjusting to the balance any easier.

We soon discovered that London is extraordinarily well set up for cyclists. As we headed South East we mostly had use of either a cycle lane or a dedicated path. Our first few glimpses of what lay ahead came in the form of Zen’s chain slipping off multiple times along the way.

Leaving the City Behind

I love boats. It’s something about being on the water that excites me. So I was delighted when we had to catch a ferry across the river at Tilbury. Getting the bikes on the boat while tricky was much easier than I had anticipated.

The further away we got from London to more beautiful the scenery became. We saw more green, fewer cars and it just seemed to be more pleasant. I pondered on the reason I had decided to spend the last three years of my life settled in London. Even more, why had I used it as a base for all of my twenties?!

Now I’m not trying to knock London. It has a hell of a lot to offer. In fact, if there is something you enjoy doing it’s happening in London. Or at the very least on the outskirts of Greater London. Perhaps it was the weather, constant grey skies, or just city life in general. But I never thought of London as home and leaving probably brought me more joy than it should have.

Lunch Time

I had spent yesterday evening marking out points along the way. The reason was for us to have a reference to how we were pacing ourselves. Given that we were attempting a 130-kilometre trek across unknown terrain it seemed like a good idea. Most of you may snark at 130 kilometres but remember I was not prepared for such a cycle. In many ways neither was my bike.

We were making reasonably good time, well for our expected pace. I had estimated we would arrive at our camp spot for 9 pm. Given the duration of the ride, a proper lunch seemed a good idea and we stopped in Rochester for the very same. Gone were the vegan options most London restaurants provided. Instead, we had to improvise by adding and removing ingredients. Unaware at the time this was something I would become very proficient at.

We have 78 kilometres remaining. What was I thinking? At least the bikes were riding strong. When we set off in the morning it seemed very plausible that they would just break apart. I felt I had to be as delicate as possible, this was changing now. They clearly handled the journey better than my legs (shaking uncontrollably) had.

It was the next half of the journey that we really started to feel the pain. We faced hill after hill and as it is the first day we resisted walking any. I say resisted because it was inevitable in the end. This slowed us down and we quickly slipped behind the mock schedule.

Wrong Turns and Old Villages

I have lost faith in the google maps cycle routes. In fairness to them, they do say they are in beta. A few points along the route the expected road turned out to be non-existent. While at others we were clearly riding on highways as cars sped past us just millimetres from the right. We have little other choices at this point but to keep following the route. Corrections will need to be made as appropriate.

The little towns we passed through on the way sparked curiosity. Coming from South Africa (a newer country), I tend to find these older villages enticing. I wonder about the lives of the people who passed before me and how their village has changed since.

Darkness is Upon Us

Everything went from exciting and new to old and tedious as the sun began to set. With less light, I wasn’t able to enjoy as much of the surroundings. The cycle seemed to become more of a race to the finish. We might have bitten off more than we can chew.

We took our last stop in Faversham as we settled into a local chain pub for some dinner. With vegan options, winning! After resting for a moment all the aches and pains I had hoped would not appear, appeared. The worst of which was an ache in the front of my left knee. You really need your knees if you are embarking on a 2000 something kilometre journey. Especially when you’re only on day one!

I’m not sure if it was the exhaustion, the dark or the sheer amount of hills we were climbing. But something made the last fifth of this ride seem harder and longer than all the others combined.

Gear Failure and Duct Tape Fixes

My bike, Zen, started to show difficulty shifting gears before the front derailleur failed completely. I complained about my limited gear ratios only to find out that Dave had the same problem ever since we left my old house. With my eyes on the prize, getting to the campsite, it seemed wise not to tinker right then.

Somehow, almost miraculously I noticed Dave’s rear-right pannier bag had ripped down the bottom seam. It was then that I heard about an early crash that I had missed. What planet was I on to miss a crash with my only partner?!

Slipped chains, flat tires, failed brakes, sure. But the last thing we expected was problems with our German made pannier bags. Each carrying 5-year guarantees. This was a harsh blow! These bags were waterproof and our only protection against the elements. Without that waterproof seal, there could be problems with wet gear.

Fear not! To fix any problem you need one of two things. Duct tape if it moves and shouldn’t. WD40 or Q20 if it doesn’t move and should. In this case, we opted for the duct tape as it was also water resistant. A quick DIY patch was made and we hoped for the best as we headed on into the distance.

Hitting the Wall

The final stretch was a killer! We faced dark, off-road paths which we could not cycle. The cold had become unbearable and we were constantly getting lost. Could this really be day one? My legs were aching like mad. I could no longer shift into my lowest gears. The balance of my bike had seemingly got worse as I began to notice more and more of a tail wag. How could we possibly make another thirty days of this?

Against all odds, we arrived at our campsite just before midnight. We moved fast, unpacking only the essentials as we prepared for bed. The wind was strong. It seemed to laugh at us as we were served our final struggle for the day. Once inside the tent, despite it all, I slept like a baby. 130 kilometres in one day, that is most definitely a record for me. Perhaps if I can survive this the rest won’t be so bad after all!
