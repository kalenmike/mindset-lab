Day Eight: The Reality of Solo Cycle Touring in the Rain

Trading companionship for independence, surviving icy roads, and fixing my first flat tire as the solo leg of the trip begins.

The wind was blowing 22km/h from the South which meant I was against it all day. I was wet and so was everything else. Strangely the cycling was turning out to be the easiest part of the journey.

The alarm buzzed early on day 7, I was on the top bunk and as the ladder was blocked by the bed we turned up I had to fling myself over the edge and shut it off. There’s no snooze needed after that activity I tell you!

It took me longer than usual to get ready because of some changes I had made to my packing and there was even a moment I feared it wouldn’t all fit in. When I look at what I have left of my possessions laid out on the floor it looks like very little but trying to fit it all into bags and on the bike makes me think I have too much.

There was a moment of apprehension and we discussed changing plans again and continuing the journey together, but we felt that the wheels were already in motion and we should continue on as planned. Going off on my own scared me so much I felt I had no choice but to do it.

I spent 20 minutes or so fine tuning my brakes and gears after the cable swap the days before and found the bike to be much more responsive, a good thing as the roads were still covered in ice and snow and I needed all the stopping power I could muster. Luckily the storm had passed over us and it was shaping up to be a beautiful day with blue skies and sunshine. The weather really does change quick!

In all honesty there couldn’t have been a better day to make my first few kilometres alone. The views were iconic and the melting snow coupled with the green fields really made me feel at ease.

After a coffee break at a local cafe, lunch on the side of the road, peanut butter on a baguette and lots and lots of cycling, I managed to find a semi dry plot of land to camp.

I was cautious not to disturb the neighbours who seemed to be close by and used my red light torch to construct the tent and cook up some dinner on my newly purchased camp stove.

It gets dark really quick in winter when you have no electricity and I was blessed with an amazing view of the stars, not quite outdoor country but much more than I had seen in a long time. I slept like a baby.

Day 8: I woke up early as I must have gone to bed really early the night before. The pitter patter of the rain drops was soothing yet disturbing at the same time. A meditation session helped and before I knew it, it was time to start packing up.

I was in no rush and took my time with breakfast and a coffee before drying off my tent and packing it up. It seemed peaceful for the most part until I was starting to be surrounded by farmers in their tractors. This gave me the oomph I really needed to get out of there!

It took me about 6km to get back onto the road heading South. Everything really looks different in the daylight. The roads were quiet and it was raining on and off, not heavy but enough to saturate you with water over a period of time. To prevent me from losing hope and falling into dispair the Sun would pop out just often enough to put a smile on my face.

Then all of a sudden the road got really bumpy and I lost control of my steering. I had my first flat tire. It was the front tire which made it easy to remove and fix, thank you quick release! It seemed to rain harder while I was patching the puncture and by the time it was replaced I was ready for a coffee and a place to dry off.

The nearest town gave me shelter at a local pub where I enjoyed a grande cafe and a cheeky bowl of fries. I asked for some tap water to replenish my stock and headed back out to make up my lost kilometres.

After cycling in the rain most of the day I was buzzing with joy when I found an isolated camp spot and pitched a tent. It’s time to dry off, eat up and get warm.

In total I managed 51km today despite the wind. Adding that to the 77km from yesterday puts me at 374km and 1 week on the road. The freedom is amazing, the fear of the unknown is taking some time to get used to.
