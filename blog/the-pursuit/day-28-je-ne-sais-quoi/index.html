Day Twenty-Eight: A Wet New Year's Eve and Surviving the Road to Bordeaux

Rethinking camp setups in wine country, foraging for chestnuts in the rain, and reaching the halfway point of the journey to Portugal.

Rations were almost depleted and every shop I came across was closed for the holidays. This grand miscalculation would end up costing me, yet there was nothing I could do about it now. The lighter weight and easier packing came with dread and uncertainty. Its funny, the grass is always greener on the other side.

Setting out the morning of day 23 I found myself deciding I was going to camp the remaining nights until I got into Bordeaux. I was planning on meeting an old traveller friend of mine and I wanted to rack up the outdoor experience before life got too comfortable. I was starting to feel guilty about sleeping indoors, it just seemed too easy.

I headed slightly off course in order to make it into Poitiers as I needed to get some banking done and my banks branches were scarce in France. I was pleasantly surprised to find that the town had a certain ‘je ne sais quoi’ about it. Maybe it was the architecture, maybe it was the hills and the view they offered but I found something moving about being there. This was almost enough to make up for the fact that my bank branch had shut down 3 days earlier, permanently. Onward and upwards!

Many thoughts ran through my mind as the sun began to set. Should I get a hotel, should I try for a bed through Warmshowers, should I try for a camp spot. Up until this point I hadn’t had much luck with city camping. I felt uncomfortable camping close to civilisation, it seemed less wild and more vagrant. I must have seemed like a deer in the headlights as a father and his son offered me help with directions. Was I going to break my pact to camp until Bordeaux on the very same day I made it?!

I retreated to the familiar McDonalds. Here I could hide from the decision and using the free WiFi to test out some ideas I had for my website. I ended up staying until just before closing. I may have missed an opportunity as a curious shopper questioned me about my bike and my sleeping arrangements. I definitely have some trust issues to work on as I answered all questions intending not to give too much away.

Before leaving the warmth and comfort of my booth I did a quick google maps scan of the local area and found what appeared to be some thick vegetation nearby. It was going to have to do. The camping spot was less than ideal and while it was off the main roads it was still in sight of a dirt road which was used a few times while I was constructing my home. This would be a quick nights sleep, no dinner and no breakfast, I was going in and out.

The rush to leave the next morning meant I ended up battling the wind while I cooked some breakfast on the side of the road further down my route. Despite the inconvenience of the last 8 hours I had finally camped out in a populated area and my confidence was high. My dependency on hotels for WiFi and electricity was lifting. I spent the day feeling very proud and excited about the possibilities this new-found confidence would bring me.

The weather was reported to be getting worse and the forecast showed some heavy rain on the way. My tent being still damp didn’t seem up to the task of keeping me dry. I found a small little patch of land where hunting was marked forbidden and re-purposed my ground sheet into a poorly tied a-frame. The idea was to allow my tent to dry out despite this expected rain. Unfortunately the night brought the most powerful wind I had felt so far and one of my pegs came loose which meant everything got wet again. My plan had failed. I knew then that I needed to learn some better knots, more rope tension could have prevented this from happening.

I left late the next morning after spending some time trying out some different tarp configurations and knots. My oats were finished and breakfast was coffee and bananas, the quinoa was headed the same direction. It was time to go shopping.

I was entering wine country and with it came constant climbs followed by steep downhills. I couldn’t help but think why I should climb the hill when I was just gonna go down the other side and do it again. I was grumpy and frustrated and in this moment it seemed like everything was against me. As if the world, for millions of years, had conspired against me to make the terrain particularly unpleasant for this journey right now. The arrogance!

Despite this I had found that lately my patience and frustrations with the road were improving. I had come to the realisation that no matter what I planned or expected it was highly likely that things would not pan out that way. I would have to learn to adapt to all situations, to bend the outcome to my desire. A simple concept for most but it really has offered more peace to my journey. I began expecting the worst while still hoping for the best and accepting that the weather had nothing against me. It would also not do me any favours! If I opened one bag what I needed would be likely be in the other. I would have mechanical troubles and flat tires in the bad weather rather than the good. When you start to realise that the world does not have anything against you, that things are just the way they are, that is when it becomes a better place to live in.

It was getting dark, I had run out of water and there was no place in sight to camp out. I decided to trust the road, not to stress and just to keep on cycling until what I needed came about. After riding through a small village I stumbled across an old water pump. Thankfully this fully restocked my water supplies and allowed me to do a little washing up. The weather was looking dry and the forecast said it would stay that way for the night. I found an isolated spot, erected my tent along with a washing line to dry my towels and began cooking the remains of my food. I got to camp with my tent open which meant no condensation, this was a welcomed relief.

The morning of day 26 the sky was blue and I was in for a bright day. My head was bursting with ideas for new camp setups and different knot types, My goal was to try to replace my tent with only a tarp, purely to see if I could hack it. Working with the rope had also given me the urge to make my own hammock, it could double as a chair or a bed when good weather came around. Most of my ride was spent with thoughts of such things and the creative juices blocked out the moment more than I would have liked.

I stopped in a small town for lunch and purchased some fresh vegetables and fruit, the other shops were all closed expect for a small bakery. The night would bring the new year and I wanted to have some treats to celebrate. Stopping for a quick snack, a local women approached me and warned me of some very bad weather approaching. I looked up at the blue skies, felt the sun on my face and dismissed the comment with a polite thank you and goodbye. This moment played on my mind for another hour or so. Was there a mistake in her translation or did she just mean the winter weather in general?!

It turned out a storm was approaching and just before dark the weather took a nasty turn for the worst. A frantic rush for cover meant I had to pass some wild walnut trees which was a real disappointment especially due to my low rations. I made it to my elected camp spot and expecting to be stuck there for the next two days I tied up my new and improved tarp layout. I wanted to give myself some cover outside the tent to feel less confined during the storm, plus it was new years.

My new years celebrations were the calmest they have been in years. I found myself alone in the forest listening to the awesome power of nature and the odd grunt echoing in the forest as the wild pigs foraged for truffles. I fantasised that the rain was washing away 2017 and that 2018 would bring a fresh start.

The next morning after making up a fresh fruit salad I was optimistic about the day and decided to pack up and journey on instead of staying put. My tent was for the most part dry, as was my gear. Tarp take two was a success! Mostly thanks to the truckers hitch, my now second favourite knot after the rolling hitch. It’s hard to really appreciate knots until you see what some of them can do.

On the way out the forest the rain started to pick up but I still found time to collect some chestnuts. I planned on cooking these up for dinner at some point. After gathering enough to make a decent meal I headed South. The road became continuously more bumpy as it took me a little while to notice that my rear tire had gone flat. It was not fully flat indicating a slow puncture and with all the rain I opted to pump it up and see if it continued to deflate. Maybe I could do a repair at my friend’s place, hopefully under shelter.

The rain started to get really bad and I was dripping head to toe, Zen had picked up an awful squeal and my boots were full of water. Coming across a bridge I decided to camp out for an hour or so and dry up with a coffee. The bridge was creating a wind tunnel which aided drying, but I was out of food and my clothes were still mostly wet. My best solution was to get into a big town. I had to resupply and dry up. I knew that being wet could end up causing more trouble that it was worth. I couldn’t risk getting sick on this trip.

It was a desperate push to close the remaining 70 kilometres into Bordeaux. The sun was already setting and this was the latest I would be out cycling since my first long day from London to Dover. It was going to be a night cycle. I arrived around 10 pm and a clean shower followed welcomed dinner. This would be the first time I could hold real conversations in the last 20 or so days, how strange.

I’m now warm and dry, my clothes are freshly cleaned and supplies are stocked up. Bordeaux has offered me sanctuary for the last 2 days and I can hear the open road calling me. With Spain just around the corner I’m sensing good things to come. I’ve now passed the 1000 kilometres mark which puts me about half way to Lisbon and will with so much still to learn and experience I’m sure it’s going to continue to be an interesting adventure.
