Day Thirteen - Reality Hits Home

Doubt weighs heavy on my mind. Everything is wet and my waterproof jacket has failed me. Even when its not raining its damp, humid and nothing seems to want to dry. My fingers are red and I can tell they’re hating the cold. Am I cut out to continue this journey or am I beat?! Lets backtrack a few days and I’ll explain how I got to this point.

The morning of day 9 started off calm, I was well hidden and felt no need to rush before sunrise so I took my time. After cooking up some oats and raisins with a fresh cup of coffee I began the daily ritual of drying off and packing up my gear. I was starting to feel for Zen who’s been left out in the rain multiple nights now, I need that grease to stay where it is, I need to consider the welfare of my machine. Maybe I should get her a tent?

Heading out the forest with Zen all loaded up was trickier than coming in. There was a steady rain and I just wanted to make a move on and forget about the fact that most of my gear was packed away wet. I reached the road hopped on the seat and low and behold, another flat tire. Luckily I still had some coffee left in my flask.

I pushed Zen a little up the road to get shelter from a tree and removed her front wheel. This flat was my fault. There was a new puncture but it was in a very similar position to the one from yesterday. On further inspection I found that I had not removed the thorn from the tire itself, rookie error! Laughing at my mistake I began whistling and gained the attention of the local farmer who’s tree I was using for shelter. He attempted to talk to me but with my limited french we ended up speaking mostly in body language with multiple thumbs up from my side. He promptly disappeared before returning with a freshly boiled cup of tea, two cubes of sugar and a small biscuit. I was impressed with this French hospitality!

With Zen back together and my belly warmed I thanked the farmer with a polite “Merci beaucoup” and wished him well with “Au revoir” before making my way up the road. “Right”, I said to myself, “NOW, we can begin”. After riding for a couple of hours it seemed the right day to make some adjustments to Zen. I made a pit stop and moved her seat a little back, dropped the height slightly and did some further fine tuning. It felt really good riding without her 20 something kilogram load, I felt powerful if not slightly off balance.

I didn’t manage to cover much distance over the day due to the constant rain which eventually had me seeking shelter in a local pub where I enjoyed not one by two coffees. Making my way out I hoped they wouldn’t notice the massive puddle of water I had left behind me imagining them cursing the foreigners once I had gone.

The positive side of this rather wet day was that I ended up finding my best camp spot so far. It was fully secluded, the ground was padded with fallen leaves and I was a little sheltered by trees. The rain stopped so I set up my tent hoping to let it air dry a little. Packing it wet meant that even the sleeping area was not fully dry. I got a good 20 minutes of dry time before the rain kicked back in. “Ah, why do you do this to me?”

I fell asleep quikly which caused me to wake up around midnight. I played some cards and did some planning before falling back to sleep a couple of hours later. Unfortunately this disruption caused me to sleep through my alarm and I woke up around 10am the next morning. It was raining all night, the spot was secluded and I thought it might help to dry out my gear, so I decided to stay put for the day and leave the next morning.

Most of day 10 was spent inside my tent hiding from the rain and I only headed out when nature called. Mostly I wrote in my notebook and thought about life while trying to stay warm and dry.

On day 11 I was up bright and early at 4am, I had some beans in tomato sauce for breakfast and my staple morning coffee before setting off. Nothing had dried and for the most part my gear was still wet. I think the ground sheet of my tent is failing and letting water through, it may be time for a tarp?! I’m sensing a lot of frustration inside myself and I am losing patience over the smallest things. Being wet is making it hard to sleep and I keep waking up from the cold. To top it off I am on my reserve tank of gas and out of food and water. Time to refuel!

I headed to the nearest camping shop where I could buy some extra stove gas and some warmer sleeping clothes. The whole day was about getting there but passing by a Mc Donalds tempted me and I went off route for the free wifi and a coffee. I didn’t know it at the time but this would cost me later.

By the time I made it to the camping shop it was already getting dark and I had no plans of where to sleep. The weather was, well, raining and so far on my journey I hadn’t attempted to search in the darkness for a camping spot. I made my purchases and decided the best way forward would be to continue towards Lisbon and stop when I saw someplace suitable to rest.

Nowhere was suitable! The ground was either extremely lumpy or covered in sharp, spiky weeds. All the land was sloped and there were no flat sections of forest. On top of that any ‘hidden’ spots would be visible as soon as the light hit. I carried on for a further 2 hours before I started to worry that I might run out of battery on my torches. I would have to settle. I found the least slopped section with reasonable coverage and camped there.

It was a nightmare, it rained all night. My tent was so wet it was raining inside and I kept sliding down the the bottom of the tent. I hadn’t eaten dinner and I told myself I just needed a short nap and I would set off first thing in the morning. Despite being so wet my new sleeping gear did keep me reasonably warm compared to other dry nights, you do try and find solace in the small things!

The morning came with a “Beep Beep Beep” and I struggle to get out of my sleeping bag to shut off the noise. It was pitch black outside and not raining! The stars were beautiful through the tree tops and I took a moment to appreciate nature, her stunning beauty which seemed to rival her ferocious inclinations. There was no breakfast and I hurried to get out of my least suitable camp spot so far before light came and revealed my location.

So far it had been dry and the weather report showed a sunny day. I was excited for the change and thought I would stop off at a local park and dry off my gear around lunch. Almost as I had this thought a great frost wind swept over me and froze everything solid, even Zen’s gears wouldn’t work for a moment. I rushed to close up all my zips and tighten my elastics to keep the warmth in my clothes. There was no feeling in my hands and toes and I pondered at what temperature frost bite would occur, maybe I was just being silly.

As we must I soldiered on, or tried too. It couldn’t have been colder than -5 Celsius but having been out all night it felt like the coldest I have ever been. I found shelter under a bridge about 5 km’s down the road and I cooked up some oats and made some coffee while I waited for sunrise.

It was in this moment that I felt truly lost. Cooking oats under a bridge while cars passed above me. It made me feel unworthy in some way. This wasn’t the romantic outdoors, this was, um, homelessness. My tent was so wet it was dripping, my sleeping bag could barely fit it in its bag and my AquaDry jacket was now AquaWet which left me without a protective barrier. “What am I doing?”, I asked myself, “how did I get myself into this position?”. Everything I now owned was packed onto the back of this bicycle I called Zen. I have no home and no idea where I am going or what I am going towards. “What is my future?”

In that moment I could have easily given up. I could have headed to an airport and flown home. It was the sheer realisation that I had no home, that I had nowhere to fly to, it was that fact and that alone that resolved me to continue. I had to see this through and hopefully find myself. Saying that, I would take a small break I deserved that much and my gear needed to dry off anyway. I hoped on my emergency 4G and booked into the cheapest motel I could find which was 70 km’s away.

It turned out to be a long hard day of cycling, probably my hardest so far. I skipped lunch, took few breaks, battled icy roads and dodged swerving cars. All in an attempt to make it to this motel and regroup.

I am now sitting in a nearby Mc Donalds preparing to set off tomorrow. My gear is all dry and reproofed with waterproofing spray, I am warm and feeling optimistic. I feel ready to continue to brave this road further at least for now.

Will I make it the remaining 1400km (as the crow flies) or will this road end up breaking me? I can’t say for certain which way this pursuit will go, but I can only hope that in this, one of my greatest challenges, I find some semblance of meaning which guides me into the future.
