Day 6: Snowstorms, Broken Gear, and Going Solo in France

Sub-zero temperatures, failing stoves, and trail-side arguments test our limits as a two-man cycle tour starts to unravel in the snow.

Snow made a scraping noise as it slid off the side of my tent. The alarm was buzzing alerting us its time to pack up camp to avoid detection. I sat up and watched the steam come off my breathe thinking ‘Wow, this is intense!’

We haven’t covered much ground over the last 4 days, some essential repairs on Dave set us back and caused us to stay in Calais an extra night than intended. It wasn’t bad though we were in a motel. We had hot water, heating and wifi. Things could have easily been worse.

The majority of day 3 was spent replacing some parts of Dave’s drive train which turned out to have more problems than originally expected. We were forced to do this in the cold for lack of a better space which slowed us down considerably. The job ended up unfinished as we headed in for a warm meal and drink.

Day 4 was an early start, we planned to cover some ground and make up for lost time. While still cold the sky was blue and it was a beautiful day. The perfect day for cycling. The weather showed snow for the next day so we knew it wouldn’t last. The ride started off good but then we started to see steeper ups and downs which forced us to stop in a forest and do some work on Dave’s brakes. Replacing the pads and fine tuning resolved the issues but it cost us valuable daylight. These things always end up being more finicky than you expect.

The beauty of the forest was magnified by the sun that shone on just the right angle to hit the trees and make them glow. The absolute silence added to the moment encouraging a peaceful mind. It was cold but not too cold, rather the type of cold that makes you appreciate your own warmth. It seemed the perfect place for a meditation session, and indeed it was.

We headed out cycling until dark, heading South and navigating purely by the sun. There was something romantic about using the sun as a navigation aid however this method did cause us to take a less than direct route. We scouted 4 sites before finally finding ground that was equally dry and secluded enough for wild camping. We set up camp taking care not to upset any locals or damage anything. We had only managed 30km for the day, but this wasn’t a race and I had all the time in the world.

A coffee would have been the perfect companion at this point and so I broke out the trusty Roben Firefly stove and started boiling some water. Old Roben Firefly was not so trusty after all and I just barely managed to warm up some water after a good 20 minutes of cook time. I thought it might have just been because the gas was so cold and accepted my warm coffee before climbing into my sleeping bag with a book and a headlamp.

It wasn’t long and we were both out cold, literally cold. It rained most of the night and began snowing early the next morning. My feet were cold all night but luckily the rest of me remained adequately warm. I must have been touching the bottom sides of my tent with my sleeping bag and bringing in the cold into my toes.

Day 5 started off with a bang and I was determined to head out before light so that we would not be seen. I packed my gear leaving my tent standing and attempted to boil some water. This time I had spent the last hour in bed with my gas canister heating it up. The stove lit, burned for a second and immediately died. I could not relight it after this. Something was wrong.

I had already been out the tent twice by this point and I was conscious of all the snow and ice I was bringing back into the tent so after dismantling most of my stove and realising I needed more tools off my bike to finish the job I decided to settle for no coffee and just drank cold water instead.

The weather was bad! There was a strong wind, very little visibility and it was snowing. We opted to wait out the storm before moving on. Inevitably I fell back to sleep and eventually woke up once it was quieter outside. We managed to pack up our gear and clear camp just as it started raining again. The puddles of water left behind were the tents were showed the extent of our problems.

Tensions were up and we had a small argument about continuing the journey together before opting to move on to the nearest town to regroup. This meant heading hard West completely off our intended course. It looked like we were facing a storm and the winds were reported to be up to 77km/h. There were points along our journey that we just couldn’t cycle or even walk but instead just stood on one side of the bikes leaning into the wind bracing as much as possible and taking a couple steps as it subsided before repeating the same again. The wind was strong and it was scary at one point it lifted me and my bike back a few centimeters. Being high up on a hill surrounded by open fields didn’t help the matter. I couldn’t help but giggle at the difficulty involved in moving forward.

We saw that the weather was meant to be getting worse the next day before clearing up for Tuesday and decided to head back indoors and wait it out. We booked the cheapest motel we could find for two nights. Which turned out to be perfect as it had a bunk bed and a single bed off to the side. We raised the single bed up into a corner creating ample work space for bike maintenance.

I spent most of the night working on Zen. I wrapped her bars with new tape for comfort, replaced the gear and brake cables and gave a final tune up. All parts these parts I had been carrying for emergencies but I decided to use them and clear some room for food in my panniers.

The recent events had caused some dissent in the group. The journey was looking much more difficult than we had thought and difficult challenges always bring up emotions. My travel companion decided he wanted some time to think about what he was doing. We agreed to part ways later in the week while he went to a meditation camp to decide what he wanted to do going forward. I would carry on my route and he may join me later if that he felt he wanted to continue on. For now we would wait out the storm together.

I woke up this morning feeling fresh and ready to go, but knowing full well I would be stuck in Saint-Martin-Boulogne for another day and night. With 40km/h winds, snow and sleet this was not a good day for cycling. At least the weather is set to be clear tomorrow. The time stuck indoors gives me the opportunity to dry out my gear, service my bike and of course update you readers. It is much appreciated time.

Now 6 days in and we have travelled 130km from London to Dover, 80km from Dover to Calais by Ferry, 30km South from Calais to Alincthun and then 15km West to Saint-Martin-Boulogne. We have covered a distance of 255km with 175km by bicycle. This puts us at 29km per day on average far below my goal of 66km. It is important to remember that this is not a race and that the lessons come in the journey not at the finish line.

My spirits are high and I’m excited to carry on braving this weather which looks to get better as I head South. Travelling alone does not scare me even though it will change the dynamic of the trip so far. Even in the most trialing of moments there are glimmers of hope to get you through. All you need is love.
