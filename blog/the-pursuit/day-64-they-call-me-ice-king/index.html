Day Sixty-Four: They Call Me the Ice King

Blasting through snow-covered roads, surviving an exploding inner tube outside a gas station, and finding unexpected peace in a Galician monastery.

There is life on the Camino after all. As I move closer to Santiago, the Compostela becomes more of a reality than a dream. I’m met by fellow pilgrims, more people than I have seen in all my days combined. Despite most of my route being isolated my dorm is now full. I find myself questioning whether it was, in fact, better on the lonesome road.

Before I enjoyed the silence of empty 60-bed dorms. I had an open reign in the hallways and common rooms became my garage. I’m now faced with limited space. Constant movement sounds as others struggle to find comfort in the well-used beds.

I had imagined that anyone I met on the Camino would be on a spiritual path. However, despite that we are all pilgrims there is a clear divide in the reasoning. It becomes apparent that there are many journeys being undertaken here. Not all of them are spiritual.

One woman has grown tired of the walking. Instead, she opts to take the bus from stage to stage. This turns what would be an 8-hour journey into 30 minutes. The reasoning seems to be so that she is still able to participate. Her friends continue stamping the road and enduring the endless hills without her. By the time the sun has set, they all eat dinner together as if the struggles of the day are equally shared.

I met a woman from Asturias. She has taken to the Camino to say goodbye to her beloved country. She is preparing to head to Romania for Erasmus. This final walk is the only way she could bear to leave her country behind.

There are those that make a habit of walking the Camino. The journey today is no more spiritual than the last one was. Some love the idea of collecting the stamps along the way. Then you have the silent ones, they speak little and don’t give away much. Only my imagination hints at the reasons behind there walk.

Watching has taught me not to ask why they walk. Instead, I wait for the information to be offered. The Camino is personal to some and I choose to respect that silent understanding. Some soul nourishing conversations were shared and friends have been made. No doubt I will see some of these people again. Yet I still find myself wishing for silence on occasion. I think back to early days when my thoughts alone accompanied me and I was the only pilgrim.

I hear rumours of the drunken celebrations happening near the Compostela. My vision of a peaceful holy place is replaced with busy pubs and drunken westerners. This strangles my desire to finish this Camino. I will however continue. Life has shown me to always form my own opinion. We all see life through a different lens.

Day 59

As I think back on what has happened over the last few days I find it difficult to find dividing lines. Each day has blended into the next. Mostly because I seemed to have experienced the same rainy weather, exhuasting long climbs, and similar mechanical failures day after day.

The focus of day 59 was to rebalance my rear wheel. I still needed the spoke tool to make this happen. Before I could start I needed to tend to a flat tire.

A smile formed on my face as I finally entered the bicycle shop which had been my destination for days. In broken Spanish and hand movements I signal my despair. “No tengo, vas a Navia para comprar” comes the response. He doesn’t have what I need. Its a further 16-kilometre ride to the next town.

I finally made it to Navia just before dark and got my hands on a spoke tool. Can you say amen! Riding with a wonky wheel is not only more difficult but it comes with along with the stress of further damage to other parts.

I spent the night indoors, it seemed easier.

Day 60

I woke up early to adjust my wheel and get everything balanced. It took some time and I could not do a perfect job. But it would have to do.

The day was wet with non-stop rain. Yet as I crossed a long bridge and left Asturias to enter Galicia the weather did not matter so much to me. Galicia had become a magical kingdom in my mind and I was looking forward to conquering the land.

Again I opted to stay indoors for the night. The excitement I had once felt for camping was waning in this wet and freezing weather. This gave me the opportunity to wash my dirty laundry, which now encompassed all the clothes I had.

Day 61 The day started with another flat tire; different wheel. I promised myself some better tires or puncture protectors at my next bicycle shop.

It was a testing day. It felt much more difficult to cycle. As I could not see any faults I assumed it must have been the hills or lack of energy from minimal sleep. When I spend the night indoors I end up staying up late to get some work done online. These are one of the only opportunities I have to use my laptop. The frequent McDonald’s stops I found in France does not seem to be as widely spread over here.

After a gruelling day of struggling up hills and braving the rain and freezing weather, a camp was a welcomed break. I found a covered woodland just off the main road. While it was unsuitable for a tent my hammock makes almost any terrain doable.

It was a quick pitch followed by a hurried meal. I then layered up and got into my sleeping bag. Warmth baby, that’s what I needed!

Day 62

Rain and hills. Snow and hills. Ok so it was beautiful and I definitely felt happy to be where I was. I mean who expects sunshine every day during winter (silly me).

Another flat! My hands now out of their comforting gloves are starting to freeze. Using the tools to change the inner tube is becoming difficult as a woman approaches me. Presumably to ask why I am in her driveway. I do my best to converse with her but her accent is strange. I am not able to make out much over the non-stop barking of her two large dogs. We stand for a moment in awkward silence before I get back to work.

I left really late today, it must have been around 13:30. The cold kept me wrapped up in my sleeping bag. The delay caused by the flat tire meant I couldn’t get really far.

Wet, cold and biting my tongue with anger slightly frustrated I stopped in a hostel for the night. This one had no hot water, but heating was all I really needed.

After dinner and some online work, I cleaned up my drivetrain. The difficulty riding turned out to be a binding chain. All the wet weather has washed out the lubrication from between the links. With all the challenges I hadn’t bothered to notice.

Day 63

I’m up early and determined to cover some distance. A more able cyclist could reach Santiago today. I’m happy to cover half the remaining distance. Something is driving me towards Sobrado dos Monxes.

The weather changes several times throughout the day. I see sunshine, rain, and snow. It’s hard to believe there was a time I had never touched snow. Now it seems to be a part of my daily routine. Snow is preferable to rain, its bounces off my waterproofs and I don’t get as wet. Plus the landscape is beautifully blanketed in white.

Heading down a steep hill at 45 kilometres a sudden pop spreads fear through my body. I slowly apply the brakes and careful head towards the side of the road. My rear inner tube has exploded?!

I want to get angry. I want to scream ‘Why me Lord?‘ Instead, I’m reminded of the Shaggy song and I smile. Just to my right, there is a gas station. Not only do I have a rain cover, I have an air pump. I also have a bucket to find a slow leak in my last remaining inner tube. To top it all off I have a sink and soap to clean up after the job. Now I’m more spiritual than religious, but really “Thank You, God!”.

It turns out that my rear tire had burst exposing the inner tube. These tires were old and I was expecting to replace them soon enough. I bought the bike second hand and then added another 2200 kilometres, I think they did well. I patch the tire with duct tape (this stuff is really handy!) and make my way to the closest bike shop.

The bike shop did all the work for me free of charge and I only paid for the parts. Conveniently they also improved the balance on my rear wheel and swapped out my brake pads. Then as I was leaving they gave me a free T-shirt. Oh yeah, Galician hospitality. #lovinglife

When I finally get where I am going I find the most beautiful monastery and it becomes home for the night. It’s not empty as expected but there are other pilgrims.

Day 64

Heavy snow today has me requesting an extra day from the monk in charge. He happily agrees. The day is spent with fellow pilgrims, some online work, and photography.

For a moment I find myself questioning what could make this moment better. I realise I am truly content, I don’t need anything else. Thinking back to my days in London, when I felt helpless. In those days I never could have imagined that I would be at peace again. Here in this monastery, it dawns on me that I may just be finding my way.

Day 65

Its the early morning and I’m waiting for the sun to rise and melt the ice on the road. Today I ride my final kilometres of this Camino. I expect to bear witness to the Compostela this evening. Bittersweet emotions overcome me as one chapter comes to an end and another begins.

I will update you all on my next chapter of this journey. Love and life.
